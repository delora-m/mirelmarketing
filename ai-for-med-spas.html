Practical applications

A defined workflow.
A clear owner and measure.

Each example starts with one nonclinical task and a named staff owner. The proposed scope depends on what the clinic’s systems support, what information can be used and which decisions require human review.

01 / RECEPTION

First response

Use clinic-approved information for routine questions about hours, location or booking. A designated reception owner reviews unanswered or uncertain inquiries and routes medical questions to qualified staff.

Measure: response coverage · first-response time · escalations
02 / INQUIRY FOLLOW-UP

Inquiry follow-up

Flag open conversations for the person responsible for follow-up. That person confirms the next action using the clinic’s communication preferences and contact permissions.

Measure: overdue follow-ups · booking completion · staff review
03 / BOOKING

Booking coordination

Use supported scheduling options and approved confirmations for routine appointments. The scheduling team handles conflicts, requests outside standard rules and unconfirmed bookings.

Measure: completed bookings · attendance · scheduling exceptions
04 / REBOOKING

Appropriate rebooking

Surface follow-up tasks based on clinic-approved criteria and communication permissions. The responsible staff member confirms whether contact is appropriate and owns the next step.

Measure: appropriate rebooking · attended return visits · staff time
05 / TEAM OPERATIONS

Administrative support

Organize approved internal information or summarize routine tasks for a team lead to review. Limit access to the information needed for the task and assign ownership of corrections.

Measure: staff time per task · correction rate · manual rework
06 / INTELLIGENCE

Revenue visibility

Bring authorized booking, visit and collections data into a review the operator can use. Assign a reporting owner to reconcile discrepancies and flag gaps before drawing conclusions.

Measure: data completeness · reconciliation time · contribution after costs

Some workflows need only clearer staff ownership or rules-based automation. We consider generative AI when interpreting or drafting information adds enough value to justify the additional testing and oversight.

The Mirèl approach to human oversight

The investment decision

Will this workflow
be worth running?

Judge a proposed change against a clear baseline, the cost of implementation and the oversight it will require in day-to-day operation.

01 / BASELINE

What is the constraint costing?

Agree which measures describe the current problem, such as missed inquiries, unused appointment capacity or minutes of staff time per task. Record the period and data sources before testing.

02 / RELIABILITY

What will staff still need to handle?

Track exception rates, escalations and instances of human intervention. Review whether staff can handle those cases reliably at the expected volume.

03 / CONTRIBUTION

What remains after costs?

Compare incremental contribution with setup, subscriptions, monitoring and staff review costs. Record staff time and capacity benefits separately from financial returns.

Before expanding: Review the observed change against the baseline, account for other factors that may have affected it and confirm that exceptions and escalations are handled within the agreed limits.

Governance and human oversight

Protect the patient.
Protect the trust.

Medical aesthetics combines commercial operations with sensitive decisions. Each workflow needs clear boundaries, named responsibilities and a review of the requirements that apply to the clinic.

  • Clinical diagnosis, treatment suitability, medical recommendations and urgent patient concerns belong with qualified professionals.
  • Review what patient information an AI tool may access, which actions it may take, who can view the information, how long it is retained, and what safeguards and vendor responsibilities apply.
  • Where HIPAA applies, evaluate business associate arrangements and required safeguards for vendors processing protected health information.
  • Check local email, SMS and telephone marketing requirements, consent, opt-out and any automated-contact restrictions.
  • Use accurate, clinic-approved content. Keep outbound messages within the information and actions approved for that workflow.
  • Test inaccurate outputs, privacy leaks, incorrect bookings and failed handoffs. Assign who reviews exceptions, when humans must step in and how the clinic continues manually.

Review the clinic’s obligations with qualified advisers before implementation. The assessment should consider the jurisdiction, provider status, intended use, vendor responsibilities and data flow alongside any vendor documentation.

Before implementation

What should you know
before approving a workflow?

Clarify the business case, the systems involved, the team’s responsibilities and the conditions for continuing before a workflow goes live.

Does every established med spa need an AI receptionist?

Reception coverage, clear ownership or rules-based automation may solve the underlying problem with less cost and oversight. We assess the constraint first and consider AI when it adds value to the specific task.

Can AI work with our current CRM or booking platform?

Compatibility depends on supported integrations, permitted data fields, scheduling access, security requirements and vendor terms. We verify what a proposed connection can read or change before including it in scope.

What remains with our reception team?

Staff retain ownership of exceptions, patient conversations that need judgment and decisions outside the agreed workflow. Routine administrative steps can be supported where appropriate. Clinical decisions remain with qualified professionals.

How should we assess a vendor’s HIPAA claim?

Review the intended use, data flow and vendor documentation with the clinic’s qualified advisers. Where HIPAA applies, evaluate the relevant agreements, safeguards and responsibilities for that particular workflow.

What would make an AI pilot worth continuing?

Compare results with the agreed baseline. Review the relevant commercial outcomes, contribution after costs, exception rates and staff intervention. Continue when the evidence supports the business case and the team can operate the workflow within its agreed limits.

How is AI different from rules-based automation?

Rules-based automation follows defined instructions, such as routing a form submission to a named queue. Generative AI can interpret or draft language within approved boundaries. The choice depends on the task and the review it requires.

How does AI fit into a Mirèl engagement?

AI orchestration is one of our three capability areas, alongside revenue and client journey intelligence and operational and financial intelligence. We start with the business constraint, then scope any work around suitable existing systems and approved third-party tools.
