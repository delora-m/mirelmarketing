The investment decision

Not another subscription.
A business case.

For an established med spa, the question is not “Can this be automated?” It is “Will improving this workflow produce meaningful value, with acceptable cost and risk?”

01 / IMPACT

Where is the loss?

Start with missed demand, delays, abandoned consultations or retention gaps. Define the baseline before proposing a new tool.

02 / READINESS

Can we implement responsibly?

Confirm integrations, data access, staff ownership, clinical escalation, vendor contracts and communication permissions.

03 / ECONOMICS

Does the value outweigh the cost?

Compare incremental contribution and capacity benefits with platform fees, setup, monitoring, errors and ongoing operating costs.

Our commercial standard: AI adoption should be evaluated through booked and attended consultations, collected revenue, retention and net contribution — not chatbot message counts or automated actions alone. Any improvement must be validated rather than assumed.

Responsibility before convenience

Protect the patient.
Protect the trust.

Medical aesthetics combines commercial operations with sensitive decisions. Technology must respect that boundary — and the particular rules that apply to each clinic.

  • Clinical diagnosis, treatment suitability, medical recommendations and urgent patient concerns belong with qualified professionals.
  • Review what patient information an AI tool may access, who can view it, retention, security controls and vendor responsibilities before connecting it.
  • Where HIPAA applies, evaluate business associate arrangements and required safeguards for vendors processing protected health information.
  • Check local email, SMS and telephone marketing requirements, consent, opt-out and any automated-contact restrictions.
  • Use accurate, clinic-approved content. No invented testimonials, misleading outcomes or AI-generated clinical claims.
  • Test hallucinations, privacy leaks, escalation, incorrect bookings and failure behavior; assign ongoing human oversight.

A vendor's “HIPAA-ready” label or a generic automation template is not a substitute for the clinic's own compliance assessment. Rules vary by jurisdiction, provider status, use case and data flow.

Questions worth asking

Less AI theatre.
More operational clarity.

A well-run medical aesthetics business should understand the costs, responsibilities and success criteria of a proposed workflow before approving its deployment.

Does every established med spa need an AI receptionist?

No. If the real problem is scheduling, staffing coverage or inconsistent follow-up, simpler technology or better process ownership may be the more effective investment. We assess the constraint first.

Can AI work with our current CRM or booking platform?

Possibly. Compatibility depends on the vendor, integrations, permitted data fields, scheduling access, security requirements and contractual restrictions. Each proposed connection needs verification before it is sold as an available feature.

Could this replace our reception team?

The objective is to support your team, not to remove human care. Routine administrative steps may be assisted by AI, while empathetic conversations, exceptions and all clinical judgment retain a clear human owner.

Does an AI vendor's HIPAA claim mean we are compliant?

No. Applicability depends on the business and data involved. When HIPAA applies, the clinic and relevant vendors must evaluate required agreements, technical safeguards and each party's responsibilities. Other privacy and communications rules may also apply.

What outcomes would make an AI pilot worth continuing?

Start with baseline metrics. Consider changes in response speed, eligible appointment bookings, attendance, administrative workload, error frequency and incremental financial contribution after platform and operating costs. Evaluate the actual data rather than assuming causation.

How is AI different from ordinary automation?

Rules-based automation follows defined instructions, such as sending an authorized appointment reminder. AI can interpret or draft information within approved boundaries. Many useful workflows need only conventional automation, while some benefit from both.

Is AI part of a wider Mirèl growth partnership?

It is one of our five connected growth capabilities. The appropriate scope is determined after reviewing the clinic's acquisition, conversion, team workflows, retention, data and priorities. We do not prescribe an AI feature just to make a proposal appear more advanced.
