The same symptom can have different causes.

When bookings fall short, the cause may be limited response coverage, scheduling friction or a gap in follow-through. As a strategic business intelligence and technology partner, Mirèl examines the work behind the numbers before recommending a change.

The Operating Diagnostic

Where are revenue, time and capacity being lost?

We examine five areas to see where the clinic is losing value, what the available data can show and which gap merits a closer look.

01

Response coverage

Which qualified inquiries go unanswered or wait too long, and who owns the response when reception is occupied?

02

Booking friction

Where do scheduling steps, unclear instructions or repeated follow-ups prevent inquiries from becoming attended consultations?

03

Capacity

Where do manual tasks, cancellations or uneven scheduling consume staff time or leave appointment capacity unused?

04

Follow-through

After a consultation or visit, is the next step clear, recorded and assigned to the right person?

05

Contribution

Which services and workflows contribute most after variable delivery costs, and which are worth investigating further?

The diagnostic establishes what can be measured now, where the data is incomplete and what we would need to learn before recommending a change.

The Mirèl Method

From diagnosis to a measurable change.

We agree the workflow to improve, the business outcome to measure and who will own the change before implementation begins.

01Establish the baseline

Diagnose

Map the current workflow, including the people, systems and handoffs involved. Review the available data, establish a baseline and identify where revenue, staff time or capacity is being lost.

Working outputA workflow map, baseline measures and a record of the main constraints and data gaps.
02Choose what to test

Prioritize

Compare likely business value, implementation cost, risk and demands on the team. Select the smallest viable change and agree who owns it, what success would look like and how it will be tested.

Working outputAn agreed scope, workflow owner, success measures and test plan.
03Test before launch

Implement

Coordinate the approved change with the team and supported systems. Define access permissions, escalation rules and the manual fallback. Test routine cases and exceptions with the people who will run the workflow.

Working outputA tested workflow, documented permissions, named owners and agreed launch criteria.
04Review the evidence

Optimize

Compare outcomes, exceptions, human intervention and operating costs with the baseline. Review what else changed during the test and use the evidence to decide what to refine, expand or stop.

Working outputA performance review with observed results, remaining constraints and recommended next steps.

Commercial Accountability

Measure the change. Account for the cost.

Before work begins, we agree the baseline, review period and measures that will inform a decision. Depending on the constraint, those might include attended consultations, capacity utilization, staff time per task or contribution after costs.

01 / Establish

A credible baseline

Record current volumes, capacity, workload and relevant costs over an agreed period. Document missing data and operating conditions that could affect the comparison.

02 / Evaluate

Incremental contribution

Where the evidence supports additional revenue from the change, deduct the variable costs of delivering it. Track changes in staff time and capacity separately.

03 / Decide

Net commercial value

Account for implementation and recurring costs, including software, monitoring and staff review. Assess whether the workflow delivers enough value to keep, adjust or expand.

Incremental contribution − additional implementation and operating costs = net financial impact

Count each cost once. The review records other changes that may have influenced performance, the quality of the evidence and any uncertainty in attributing the outcome.
