Illustrative specification · example only

After-hours booking inquiry

A design example, not a deployed product or a client result.

Trigger
A routine booking question arrives through one agreed inquiry channel after reception closes.
Approved source
Clinic-approved booking guidance and the current booking link.
AI task
Prepare a response draft from that guidance and flag missing or uncertain information.
Human approval
The reception lead reviews and sends the draft in this proposed workflow.
Next owner
An unresolved inquiry remains assigned to reception until a booking or another outcome is recorded.
Exception route
Questions about treatment suitability go to a qualified clinician. Unsupported requests go to staff review.
Manual fallback
If a system connection fails, the inquiry remains in the source channel for the reception team to handle.
Review measures
Time to the first response actually sent, attended consultations, staff intervention and operating costs.

Before implementation: confirm system compatibility, access, responsibilities, test cases and acceptance criteria.

Explore how we work
Where should we start if we are unsure what is wrong?

Bring one recurring issue to the Strategy Review: unanswered inquiries, repeated administration, a reporting gap or a difficult handoff. We discuss what you are seeing and whether a separately scoped diagnostic makes sense.

Will we need to replace our booking software or CRM?

We start with your existing systems. Supported integrations, vendor terms and permitted access determine what is feasible. A change in tools is considered when its expected value justifies the cost and disruption.

Does every engagement include AI?

No. Clearer responsibilities, a process change or rules-based automation may address the constraint. AI is considered where it adds enough value to justify testing, operating cost and human review.

Can we start with a diagnostic without committing to a build?

Yes. A diagnostic is a defined stage with its own scope and outputs. Any design, implementation or ongoing support is agreed separately before that work begins.

How are fees, timing and responsibilities set?

The proposal specifies the deliverables, required access, client responsibilities, milestones and fees. It also identifies third-party costs and whether monitoring or ongoing support is included. The scope depends on the problem and the systems involved.

How will we judge whether the work is paying off?

We agree a baseline, measures and a review period, then assess the observed result alongside delivery costs, running costs and other changes that may have influenced performance. A fixed financial return is not guaranteed.
