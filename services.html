Commercial Accountability

Measure the change. Account for the cost.

We agree what to measure before work begins. A booking workflow may be assessed through attended consultations and exceptions. An administrative workflow may be assessed through staff time per task and the share of cases still requiring manual intervention.

We compare outcomes with the baseline, account for delivery and operating costs, and explain how confidently the change can be linked to the result. Leadership can then decide what to keep, adjust or expand.

How we evaluate business impact.

01
Establish the baseline

Record the relevant volumes, workload, capacity and costs over an agreed period. Note where data is incomplete.

02
Assess incremental contribution

Where additional revenue is attributable to the change, deduct the variable costs of delivering it. Track staff time and capacity benefits separately.

03
Evaluate the net outcome

Account for implementation, software, monitoring and staff review. Check that exceptions and escalations are handled as designed.

We agree the metrics, attribution method and review schedule for each engagement. Actual outcomes depend on the baseline, implementation and operating conditions.

Before We Begin

What to expect from an engagement.

We agree the problem to address, responsibilities, technology requirements and measures of success before work begins.

How does Mirèl work with our existing team?

We work with leadership and the staff responsible for the workflow. Together, we define what changes, who approves it, who handles exceptions and how the team will review performance. Clinical decisions remain with qualified professionals.

Will we need to replace our CRM or booking software?

We start with the systems you already use. We review supported integrations, data access and permissions, then consider a change in tools only if the expected value justifies the cost, disruption and ongoing maintenance.

Can you implement AI reception and follow-up?

We can assess and design scoped workflows for nonclinical tasks such as routing inquiries, coordinating bookings or supporting follow-up. Implementation depends on the use case, system compatibility, clinic-approved information, access permissions and testing. Staff review, escalation and fallback procedures are defined before launch.

What technology and third-party costs should we expect?

We work with suitable existing systems and approved third-party tools. Where a workflow requires new subscriptions, usage fees, integrations or ongoing monitoring, we identify those costs and responsibilities in the proposed scope.

How will we judge whether the work is paying off?

We agree a baseline, target measures and a review period. We then compare the observed results with the cost of implementing and running the workflow, accounting for other changes that may have influenced the outcome. A fixed financial return is not guaranteed.
